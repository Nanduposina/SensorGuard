# SensorGuard resume-claim audit

## Evidence standard

This audit assesses four proposed resume bullets against the current implementation, tests, measured experiments, and the [official Intel Berkeley Research Lab data page](https://db.csail.mit.edu/labdata/labdata.html). “Verified” applies only to the scope tested here. Artificially injected faults support controlled recall measurements, not historical accuracy. Unmodified readings remain unlabeled unless independently reviewed.

The official page describes 54 deployed motes, their coordinates, per-mote epoch counters, occasional missing/truncated data, and voltage variation strongly correlated with temperature. The local source has 61 observed IDs, including additional IDs; the project does not force the observed count to 54. The official [mote coordinate file](https://db.csail.mit.edu/labdata/mote_locs.txt) supplies physical positions for IDs 1–54.

## Reproducible Phase 5 evaluation

Commands used from the repository root, with the existing `.venv`:

```powershell
$python = ".venv/Scripts/python.exe"
$sensors = (1..54) -join ","
& $python scripts/run_experiment.py --input data.txt/data.txt --output-dir artifacts/phase5-baseline-20261027 --sensors $sensors --max-per-sensor 150 --seed 42 --epochs 2
& $python scripts/run_experiment.py --input data.txt/data.txt --output-dir artifacts/phase5-contextual-20261027 --sensors $sensors --max-per-sensor 150 --seed 42 --epochs 2 --contextual --peer-radius-meters 8 --peer-minimum 3 --peer-tolerance 120s
& $python scripts/compare_contextual.py --baseline-dir artifacts/phase5-baseline-20261027 --contextual-dir artifacts/phase5-contextual-20261027 --output artifacts/phase5-contextual-comparison-20261027.csv
```

The runner hashed the source and verified identical sampled rows and fault manifests before comparison. It selected up to 150 source-order readings for each of motes 1–54, retained 7,693 rows in the dense time window, then split chronologically into 4,615 train, 1,539 validation, and 1,539 held-out test rows. Three synthetic faults modified 11 feature-reading pairs. The unit is a sensor-feature-reading pair (with a `record` marker for record-level faults). Metrics count each alert pair once. Models were fit on unmodified training rows; thresholds were calibrated using unmodified validation scores only; injections were applied to the held-out copy after contextual features were prepared.

| Detector | Baseline injected recall | Baseline alerts / rate on unlabeled pairs | Contextual injected recall | Contextual alerts / rate on unlabeled pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rules | 11/11 (1.000) | 1,001 / 13.03% | 11/11 (1.000) | 1,037 / 13.50% |
| Isolation Forest | 0/11 (0.000) | 194 / 2.52% | 0/11 (0.000) | 48 / 0.62% |
| Autoencoder | 1/11 (0.091) | 302 / 3.93% | 1/11 (0.091) | 87 / 1.13% |

The contextual test had features available for 32 of the 54 selected sensors, with mean per-feature-reading availability 40.27%. The quality-rule-only recall was 5/11 in both runs; statistical-rule recall was 6/11 in both. The combined alert-volume reduction is descriptive and does not establish fewer false positives: the 7,684 non-injected pairs are unlabeled. The synthetic distribution has only three events and should not be generalized to natural faults.

## Historical battery evidence

### Separate published EV battery-fault evaluation

To evaluate documented real-world battery-fault cases without relabeling Intel Lab candidates, SensorGuard now includes a separate adapter for the brand 3 EV subset from Zhang et al., [Nature Communications (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41226-5), [Figshare DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.23659323](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.23659323). The study states that vehicle-level fault labels derive from driver reports and engineer confirmation of lithium plating, low electric range, over-temperature, or unexpected voltage behavior. This supports a published real-world vehicle-fault benchmark, not timestamped physical battery interventions. The downloadable brand 3 label file measured 100 vehicles (9 fault, 91 normal) and the data folder had 29,598 charging snippets. The archive MD5 and SHA-256, per-vehicle outputs, split assignments, and full counts are recorded in ignored artifacts under `artifacts/phase6-real-battery-ev-brand3-final2-20261027/`.

The run used 55 normal training vehicles, 18 disjoint normal validation vehicles, and a 27-vehicle held-out test group containing all 9 fault-labelled vehicles and 18 source-labelled normal vehicles. The train/validation/test snippet counts were 10,432 / 1,120 / 1,827. Training used only the first 70% of mileage-ordered snippets for training vehicles; validation and test scores each aggregate their vehicle's latest 20% of snippets. Validation-normal vehicle scores alone set each detector's 95th-percentile threshold. Per vehicle, the score is the mean of the highest-scoring 10% of its snippets.

| Detector | TP fault vehicles | FN fault vehicles | FP source-normal vehicles | TN source-normal vehicles | Precision | Recall | F1 | Vehicle FPR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Isolation Forest | 0 | 9 | 0 | 18 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Autoencoder | 4 | 5 | 4 | 14 | 0.500 | 0.444 | 0.471 | 0.222 |

The denominator for the external false-positive rate is 18 held-out vehicles labelled normal by the study. These are vehicle-level outcomes; no timestamped fault onset or snippet-level label exists, so the project does not claim an event-level detection delay or false alarms per valid reading. The source has only nine fault-labelled vehicles in this subset, and this test is not evidence of transfer to Intel Lab sensor motes. For reproduction, run `python scripts/evaluate_real_battery.py <extracted-brand3-directory> --archive <brand3.tar.gz> --output-dir <new-artifact-dir> --seed 42 --epochs 20 --estimators 200 --threshold-quantile 0.95`.

The separate Zenodo/2025 publication also describes real BMS fault classes whose labels were assigned after pack disassembly, but its GIS archive has no accompanying ID-to-label manifest that could be matched for this adapter. It was therefore not used to calculate project metrics.

No independently verified battery hardware failures, maintenance records, or battery replacement labels are present for Intel Lab in the repository or official dataset. External literature does provide retrospective fault interpretations: [Ni (2008), Figure 2.10](https://gregorypottielab.org/theses/kevin-thesis.pdf) marks an approximate voltage below which two nearby, unnamed Intel Lab motes begin to fail; [Sharma et al. (2007)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/31f765tj) describe Intel Lab temperature faults correlated with supply voltage becoming insufficient; and [Scholl et al. (2023), Section 3.7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10140861/) discuss voltage-drop effects in motes 46–48. These publications are useful evidence that voltage-related sensor degradation has been suspected in this dataset, but they do not provide battery service/replacement ground truth, and the first source does not provide mote IDs or exact event times. They cannot support observation-level supervised Intel Lab metrics. The official documentation says voltage variation is strongly correlated with temperature and that these lithium-ion cells generally maintain fairly constant voltage. The full streaming investigation evaluated 299 seven-day sensor windows; 296 crossed its exploratory adjusted-slope cutoff of −0.005 V/day. This unusually broad screen is evidence that the candidate rule is not specific enough to diagnose failures, not evidence of 296 battery events. Temperature-adjusted declines remain observational candidates: the linear adjustment is not causal and load, shared environmental/time effects, calibration, sampling gaps, or truncation can explain patterns. No Intel event-level precision or recall is supportable.

## Remaining scope and release state

The bounded Intel Lab evaluation does not establish production thresholds, natural-fault accuracy, or battery-failure detection. The external EV vehicle benchmark is an initial labelled comparison, not an Intel mote or event-level validation. Physical proximity does not prove that sensors share local conditions. Stronger Intel Lab battery evaluation requires records that identify affected mote IDs and failure/service/replacement times, plus a failure definition and independent observation-level labels. Reading-level false-alarm measurement additionally requires representative confirmed-normal intervals; reviewer selections are alert-conditioned and are not a dataset-wide normal baseline. The external benchmark would benefit from larger fault-labelled vehicle cohorts and event boundaries. The implementation was released on `main` as commit `29a0e95d791a97a83c398cadc642fb0adf7f2c0c`; its GitHub Actions run [40531097559](https://github.com/Nanduposina/SensorGuard/actions/runs/40531097559) completed successfully. The original source dataset and user-owned local files remain unmodified and untracked.
